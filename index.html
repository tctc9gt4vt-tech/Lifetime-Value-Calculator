Trade-ins, refurbished and informal used sales are worked out by following every device through its owners, so they depend on the route, resale and flow inputs. How the model works

Each year's sales in each region form a cohort, sold evenly through the year. The model follows every cohort month by month. At the end of each life, the devices split across the routes you set: traded in and refurbished, sold privately, passed on, put in a drawer, recycled or lost. Paths that end in the same place at the same time are merged, so every combination of routes, countries and owners is covered without listing them one by one.

New device sales are units × average selling price. Bundled contracts add the operator's handset subsidy as a cost. Finance interest uses an amortising loan on the financed share at the blended APR; a 0% APR mix pulls the blended rate down.

Airtime & service is devices in use × the plan-weighted ARPU of the region they are in, times the attribution %. ARPU trends by year. New and second-hand owners have separate plan mixes, so a share of second-hand devices can be unconnected.

Secondary market is the refurbisher's resale revenue, or the marketplace fee on a private sale. Resale prices come from the cohort's selling price × (1 − annual decline)^age × the destination's price index. Trade-in payouts, refurbishment, and logistics & duties into a new region are costs. Material recovery is booked at formal recycling, less collection cost.

Price tiers split each region's sales into premium, mid-range and budget handsets with their own prices. Each tier can hold its value better or worse than the global rate, and scales the first owner's keeping time, the share traded in after the first owner, the share bought on finance and the first owner's ARPU. When more or fewer devices are traded in, the other routes are scaled so the split still totals 100%. Later owners use the regional inputs whatever the tier.

History and forecast. Launch years can start as early as you like. Prices and ARPU are entered for one stated year, and their trends run backwards and forwards from it. Years up to the last year of actual data are shaded as history. Enter real volumes for those years so the realised value can be checked against reported figures. Present value discounts or compounds every year to the stated price year.

Launch years. Each year's sales are followed to the end of their last life, so the launch-year view shows the full lifetime value of, say, 2015 handsets against 2022 handsets, and how much of it had been realised by the end of the actual data.

Brands (Apple, Premium Android, Other Android, Other) split each region's sales. Each brand has its own tier mix, so the region's tier mix follows from its brand mix. A brand's price index scales the regional tier price, its value decline replaces the tier's when set, and its other multipliers are applied on top of the tier's. Scenarios saved before brands existed keep brands switched off and give the same results as before.

Inputs by year. On the Brands, Price tiers, Airtime, Buying and Routes tabs, any cell can be given values for chosen years. Years in between are interpolated in a straight line, and the value holds flat before the first and after the last year set. The first time a cell is given a year, the single value is kept as the first launch year's value so the change ramps from it. Brand, tier, price, buying and route settings follow each handset's launch year for its whole life. Plan mix and ARPU follow the calendar year the device is in use. Prices and ARPUs set by year are used as entered; the others follow their yearly trend.

Impacts are events layered on top of a scenario. Each one changes chosen levers by a percentage at full strength, with a timing shape that sets how much of that applies in each year. Sales, prices, buying, trade-in, lifetime and value-decline effects follow the launch year; ARPU effects follow the calendar year; refurbishment and logistics effects follow the launch year of the devices being handled. The impact results compare the scenario with and without its impacts, and show each impact on its own.

Comparison runs scenario B through the same engine with the same filters and shows the difference from the current scenario.

Devices still in use when tracking stops are counted as still in circulation. After the maximum number of lives, owner routes are redirected to recycled or lost in the same proportion as those two routes.
